Abstract

Text-guided video editing aims to transform a source video according to a target text prompt while preserving desired visual content and temporal coherence. Despite recent progress, existing approaches often target specific editing scenarios (e.g., zero-shot or one-shot, local or global editing), struggle to process long videos due to memory and computational constraints. In this paper, we propose UELVE, an Large Language Model-driven Unified framework for Efficient Long-Video Editing based on pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models. UELVE first exploits the reasoning capability of an LLM to automatically analyze the source and target prompts, enabling adaptive decisions on editing strategies. To support long-form video editing, we organize video frames into grids and perform sequential grid-based diffusion, where grid self-attention explicitly ensures short-term consistency among neighboring frames. Furthermore, a simple yet effective grid sliding mechanism is introduced to establish inter-grid interactions, maintaining long-term temporal consistency. In addition, UELVE naturally supports multi-GPU parallel inference with only minimal inter-GPU communication, substantially improving editing efficiency. Extensive experiments on a wide range of video editing scenarios demonstrate that UELVE consistently achieves superior editing quality, strong temporal consistency, and significantly improved efficiency compared with state-of-the-art methods.

Framework of UELVE. We first arrange the frames of input video VK into grids, encode them into the latent space and then invert the original latents to ZT. Then we denoise the grid latents ZT to Ẑ0 via T time steps of iterations on a pre-trained Text-to-Image diffusion model, decode Ẑ0 and rearrange the resulted grid frames into an edited video V̂K as output. For the denoising process, by comparing the source and target prompts, we leverage an LLM to reason out specific editing goals and types and thus make decisions on corresponding model adaptions, unifying diverse editing tasks into a same framework. Beyond the grid self-attention for within-grid interactions, we perform a grid sliding manipulation to enhance cross-grid interactions to maintain long-term consistency. Benefiting from the flexible grid manipulations, a multi-GPU data parallel computing strategy is also considered to remarkably improve the computation efficiency.

Diverse Editing Scenarios

UELVE is an efficient long-video editing method that leverages LLM to make decisions on model adaptions according to specific editing types, implements grid diffusion on a pre-trained Text-to-Image model to maintain short-term and long-term consistencies, and enhances the efficiency by multi-GPU data parallel computation. It is applicable to diverse editing tasks, including object/background replacement, local editing of attributes (style, color, shape), global style transformation and even motion editing.

Main Results

Qualitative comparison with baseline methods. Compared with one-shot methods (Video-p2p, FateZero) and zero-shot methods (RAVE, ControlVideo), UELVE (Attention and ControlNet variants) achieves better editing fidelity and temporal consistency. Quantitative comparisons and ablation studies are provided in the paper.
